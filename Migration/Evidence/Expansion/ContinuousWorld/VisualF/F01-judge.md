## Waterside — unchanged at 5.45

### Composition

- The traveler and diagonal bridge are recognizable, but the target bridge is a broad stone mass with a clearly legible arch. F01 remains a long slab strip between narrow extruded parapets; a large right-bank rock mass partly competes with the arch opening. Increase readable masonry body and arch depth, and reduce the surrounding rock dominance so the arch remains the visual anchor.
- Hero and view-48 still show the same oversized left tower and right tower/tree group. Mix low horizontal fractured shelves into the bank, vary proportions and orientation, and avoid a necklace of similarly embossed stacks.
- Both approach slabs remain rectangular pavement pads with abrupt straight ends. View-35 makes this especially obvious. Stagger, partially bury and remove selected terminal slabs so the approach dissolves into terrain instead of stopping as a precise sheet.
- Golden reeds remain sparse thin vertical marks rather than the target's wide organic shore masses. Use substantial overlapping clusters with varied height and gaps, including the foreground shoreline.
- The target's shallow submerged rock groups are not legible. Actual water is predominantly an uninterrupted teal field with shadows/reflections. Add visible shallow structure with depth-dependent visibility; submerged objects must read through water, not as dry props floating on top.

### Lighting, materials and details

- Retain appropriate sunny direction and teal family, but establish lighter luminous aqua shallows, warmer stone/reeds, and less uniformly dark bank rock. The target has gentle local water-depth gradients and coherent dark zones under the arch.
- The scattered cloudy white water patches do not match directional small ripple glints. Refine surface scale and coherent reflection/shadow placement; retain contrast without making rock shadows opaque black masses.
- Slab edges and joints remain too mechanically straight. Add local chips, inconsistent widths and stone-level wear; break long shared seam lines and vary height/roughness subtly.
- Parapets need larger irregular stone units and weathered coping rather than fine parallel courses and clean continuous edges.
- The bank rock's equally strong cracks, repeated stack forms and dark embossed creases read manufactured. Use broader planes, localized fracture hierarchy and less uniform normal contrast.
- The tree still has conspicuous pale branch sticks and opaque green canopy fragments. Improve twig/leaf breakup and soften overly exposed branch contrast.
- Reeds need bent stalks/heads, warm straw variation and grounded shoreline contact. Waterline dampness, submerged distortion and shore contacts remain secondary missing details after the large forms.

No meaningful regression is evident in these three views; minor water/weather phase differences do not justify a score change.

## Abbey — 6.15, composition improves by 0.35

### What improved

- View-180 replaces the bowed tile ribbon with a much straighter diagonal road. This is closer to the target's primary layout and is a genuine image-level improvement.
- A consistent strip of earth now separates road from the ruin. The road no longer presses against the broken corner in the same way, so interior, ruin edge and external circulation are easier to distinguish.
- Hero-abbey-west shows a relocated internal arch/roof arrangement and a more open near breach. It gives useful supporting structural evidence, but the target is an exterior view; an interior arch cannot substitute for exterior fidelity.

### Remaining composition gaps and local tradeoffs

- The road is still a precise strip of many small tiles. Compared with the target's traveler, individual paving units are too small and uniform. Increase slab dimensions and vary proportions while preserving the new straight overall alignment.
- The visible lower road end is a hard transverse row on bare earth. This has become an especially conspicuous constructed endpoint after straightening. Continue it out of the relevant view or resolve it as a natural staggered transition with embedded/broken stones; do not leave the clean rectangular cutoff.
- The broken wall remains thin and the rubble skirt consists mainly of tiny similar gray fragments. The target has large collapsed blocks and stepped broken wall sections embedded in moss, smaller debris and soil. Build a few major collapse masses first, then connect them with medium fragments and small chips across a believable width.
- There is more open separation now, but large empty uniform ground areas make sparse prop placement more obvious. Ground the road edge and ruin bases with connected damp soil, moss and restrained vegetation masses.
- The interior view's new full arch is visually prominent while the near lamp-bearing wall is reduced. This changes the balance of warm architecture and shadow; it is not automatically closer to the exterior target. Preserve strong Gothic structures while placing the broken exterior wall and warm source where they support the target-facing shot.

### Lighting, materials and details

- Cool rainy light and warm lamps remain the strongest matches; no clear lighting gain warrants added points. Road and floor remain too evenly pale, with narrow joints outlining a tidy grid.
- Replace the broad flat pale response with varied worn stone: darker wet base regions, irregular shallow puddle patches, coherent elongated highlights and local roughness variation. A few circular bright light spots and isolated rings do not establish wetness across the pavement.
- Extend lamp color as an uneven wet reflection across neighboring slabs and into local masonry. Keep the pools connected to their source instead of small clean isolated patches.
- Wall surfaces remain dark, clean and precisely coursed. Concentrate chips and moisture/weathering at breaks, ledges and bases; establish larger stone value variation and green growth on upward-facing rubble surfaces.
- Bright flat pale patches remain near the right interior wall and some wall/column bases. They still look like pasted or intersecting sheets, not grounded broken stone. Inspect the responsible geometry/material and resolve the value discontinuity and contact.
- Debris needs contact shading and dirt accumulation, with moss joining wall to ground. More gray chips alone will not produce the target's thick green collapse margin.
- Rain ripple scale/distribution should follow actual wet regions. Stills cannot validate temporal rain naturalness.

Overall this is a layout gain rather than a material-quality gain. The new hard road endpoint and continued empty strip are local issues; the total region is nevertheless closer than baseline.

## Snow hero342 — unchanged at 7.90

- Composition remains close: diagonal path, traveler, left/right framing trees and snow-covered rock groups retain the successful arrangement. Small pose/particle differences do not change the score.
- Cold blue-white light and long shadows are appropriate. Highlights are still comparatively flat/bright, and character/rock contact depth can be improved without changing the overall palette.
- Conifers remain coarse overlapping angular green plates with thick independent-looking white fragments. Replace the visibly broad plate silhouettes with finer needle/branch breakup and snow accumulation following upper branch surfaces. The target has more finely perforated crowns.
- Snow caps are still smooth inflated slabs in places. Introduce uneven accumulation thickness and wind-shaped transitions to exposed stone, with subtle granular detail; avoid merely amplifying global bump.
- The path remains a coarse mottled gravel/ice strip with small circular granular marks. The target is a softer compacted snow track. Reduce coarse pattern contrast and add broad low-amplitude compaction, softened edges and restrained local track detail.
- Add subtle boot/tree/rock contacts and small drift hollows while preserving the open smooth snow areas. No wholesale rearrangement is indicated for this particular hero.

## Separate location gates

### Overlook375 — FAIL; limited boundary improvement, unresolved structural problem

- The baseline's ruler-straight diagonal boundary has become a broad curved contour. This is a visible improvement in shape, but a discontinuity still spans the upper portion: the path abruptly meets a differently shaped/surfaced white field. It can still read as one surface laid over another. Blend geometry, normals, material and path termination together, and inspect adjacent viewpoints.
- The left bulky rock cluster has been pulled back and the right cluster reduced/moved away from the center. The approach is more open, but this exposes substantially more of the smooth gray-white right escarpment. **This is a local regression in cliff believability:** a broader blank snow wall is now visible.
- That wall has no readable stratified rock support or accumulation logic. Give the drop actual layered exposed rock, ledges and irregular snow retention instead of one continuous smoothly shaded slope/wall.
- Opening the corridor has not produced an overlook vista. No distant landscape, atmospheric layers or depth beyond the lip is legible in this capture. Establish a foreground ledge and a visible middle/far landscape relationship; repositioning foreground boulders alone is insufficient.
- The path/endpoint still needs a clear visual resolution. The waystone supplies a landmark but does not explain the drop or continuation. Static placement cannot prove the end boundary, collision or camera behavior.
- Left-side rocks/tree bases and remaining right-side boulders need grounded accumulation/contact instead of discrete props attached to a snow plane.

This location does not inherit any of snow342's score or acceptance. The changed screenshot is not enough to mark the terrain issue fixed.

### Rainwood120 — NEEDS IMPROVEMENT; visually essentially unchanged

- The same similar-height tree rows, repeated low tufts, central brown route and uniform surrounding green ground remain. No substantial new grouped canopy, landmark or ground hierarchy is visible.
- Group trees into varied overlapping masses with differing crown heights/trunk lean and intentional openings. Use connected undergrowth patches and root/leaf-litter contacts to integrate those masses.
- Introduce broad damp soil/leaf gradients and irregular path-edge erosion, not uniformly scattered little plants. Add occasional significant side features to break the repeated corridor rhythm.
- Use canopy shadows and atmosphere to make an approach toward the abbey spatially apparent. This single still cannot establish boundary continuity or weather blending.

### Frost ascent265 — NEEDS IMPROVEMENT; visually essentially unchanged

- The large bare right rock stack, near-identical dark conifers and gray central route remain. The frame still lacks persuasive gradual retained-snow distribution.
- Build patchy snow in sheltered ground and rock crevices, mixed branch accumulation, exposed wet earth and a readable increasing coverage direction. Some snow exists at the upper edge, but that alone does not resolve the broad gray corridor.
- Vary rock scale and tree spacing to form larger landforms and a changing ascent rhythm. The dominant right formation still reads as an isolated prop; some tree/boulder bases appear perched and need terrain contact.
