Reviewed 2026-09-10. Fresh judgment against the ORIGINAL concept images; no previous verdict or score was consulted. ## Principal blockers and order of work

1. **Waterside needs a different hierarchy of large forms.** The target is a broad weathered stone arch, modest parapets, varied low and high bank formations, shallow turquoise water with visible submerged rocks, and dense golden reeds. E02 is dominated by long precise pavement rectangles, extruded rail-like parapets, near-identical tall bank rock stacks, and a large opaque teal surface. Reshape these before adding small props: give the arch and its masonry body substantial readable volume; break the pavement approach into irregular individual stones; lower and spread some bank rocks into ledges; place submerged rock groups in transparent shallows; group reeds in broad irregular masses.
2. **Abbey exterior composition and surface response are both materially wrong.** The target has a broad, mostly straight diagonal stone road beside a jagged, mossy ruin edge. E02's road curves into a narrow regular tile ribbon, and the floor/road reads as bright clean uniform tiles. Rebuild the road silhouette and slab scale first, then make broken masonry/rubble a connected irregular boundary, and finally establish broad wet reflections and varied worn stone. The interior hero cannot stand in for the exterior target.
3. **The overlook has an obvious scene-construction artifact.** In view-375 a straight diagonal change of terrain shading/material cuts across the upper third of the frame. The right/bottom escarpment also reads as a smooth extruded snow wall. Fix terrain continuity and give the overlook a legible near ledge, drop and distant space before polishing foliage. Snow342's fidelity does not pass this location.
4. **The snow hero has the closest large-form match but still exposes coarse assets.** Tree silhouettes are built from broad angular plates and thick white fragments; rock snow caps look smooth and rounded. Preserve the existing hero arrangement while improving branch/needle breakup, snow accumulation, contact shading and fine surface response. Avoid moving its major features solely to compensate for material problems.
5. **Transition views are repetitive corridors.** Rainwood120 has repeated similar trees and isolated tufts on largely uniform terrain; Frost265 has a gray corridor bordered by oversize rock forms and dark similar conifers. Give each a layered spatial rhythm and a clearly visible material/ecological progression. These stills cannot prove that lighting, weather, terrain, collision or camera behavior stays continuous while traveling.

## Waterside — detailed findings

Target: `.dream-loop/continuous-world/concept-waterside.png`.

### Composition

- The main diagonal bridge and centered traveler are recognizable, and view-48 gives a useful wider view. However, the bridge is relatively narrow, the masonry body is visually thin, and its parapets read as long straight mechanically extruded rails. Increase the visual width/body of the arch and break the parapets into irregular masonry courses with substantial coping blocks; retain a traversable deck.
- The target's bridge is an integral bank-to-bank structure with a clear dark vaulted opening and softly connected approaches. E02 has square terminal pads and continuous rectilinear pavement extending far beyond the deck. Replace the square pad termination with staggered, partially embedded slabs that loosen into the approach ground.
- In the hero, the large top-left rock stack and right bank tower overwhelm the bridge. The target includes tall rocks but balances them with long low fractured shelves and more shoreline vegetation. Reduce some bank stack height, vary their proportions/orientation, and build asymmetric shelves close to the water plane instead of a sequence of separate tall masses.
- View-35 exposes a long largely bare approach and an abrupt straight pavement end. Continue the target's scattered worn stepping stones and grass/reed clusters into the ordinary approach view, not just the hero crop.
- Water lacks the target's visible submerged stone groups. Place appropriately scaled groups below the surface, with shallows that reveal their forms and deeper regions that conceal them.
- Target golden reeds occupy substantial foreground and bank areas. E02 reeds are a thin line of small vertical specks. Increase cluster width, height diversity and area coverage, keeping patches and gaps rather than a uniform edge fence.

### Lighting

- E02 has broadly suitable daylight and teal water, but the target is softer, warmer on stone and vegetation, and more luminous through the shallows. Reduce the dark green/gray weight of the bank rocks and establish a shallower aqua band near shore.
- The target's water has directional glints, local surface motion and coherent dark reflections beneath the arch and tree. E02's bright flecks are scattered cloud-like patches across a nearly flat field. Tighten the specular structure into small directional ripples and provide spatially coherent reflection/shadow regions.
- Deep hard rock/bridge shadows currently dominate the water. Preserve arch depth but recover detail and blue-green variation in those dark areas.

### Materials and details

- Deck slabs have very straight edges, dark uniform grooves and relatively identical pale color. Vary slab dimensions, chipped corners, height/normal response and joint width. Break several straight continuous joints and add subtle edge dirt without turning every joint black.
- Parapet side courses look finely lined and regular compared with the target's larger weathered stone blocks. Use larger readable masonry units and irregular coping edges.
- Repeated rock stack silhouettes and strongly embossed dark creases make bank assets look manufactured. Vary rock assets or meaningfully rotate/reshape them; mix broad planar fractures with finer weathering instead of emphasizing every crack equally.
- Tree foliage is harshly opaque green with exposed pale branch sticks. Add leaf-size variation and smaller silhouette breakup; tune branch value and canopy density toward the target's softer tree.
- Reeds need bent stalks, heads, warmer straw colors and localized water reflections/contact, rather than uniformly thin straight strokes.
- Add local submerged stone distortion, waterline darkening, shore dampness and smaller stone/grass contacts after the large forms are corrected.

## Abbey — detailed findings

Target: `.dream-loop/continuous-world/concept-ruins.png`.

### Composition

- View-180 is the relevant exterior comparison. Its bowed road and small repeated slabs differ substantially from the target's broad near-straight diagonal road with larger varied slabs. Adjust the exterior road layout and its slab scale before adding more decoration.
- The target's broken ruin edge is a substantial layered mass of large broken masonry, rubble, moss and soil beside the traveler. E02's breach is largely a low thin broken wall with a narrow gray debris skirt. Make the collapse occupy real width and height, with a few large fallen pieces anchoring a graded rubble field.
- Keep the target's separation between interior floor, broken wall/soil margin, and exterior road. The current curved pavement crowds the ruin corner and makes those zones less distinct.
- Hero-abbey-west reveals recognizable Gothic walls, columns and warm lamps, but its centered interior traveler, large regular floor expanse and largely intact perimeter are not the target exterior composition. It is supporting asset evidence only; it cannot replace view-180 for target matching.
- Tree placement and sizes need more irregularity and a closer relationship to ruin edges; repeated upright crowns currently read as spaced props.

### Lighting

- Cool rainy illumination, long structural shadows and warm lamp accents are present. These are the strongest matches in the region.
- Pavement is too uniformly pale relative to the dark walls. The target distributes darker wet stone and reflective bright patches across individual slabs. Reduce the uniform pale base and create locally brighter wet regions.
- Lamp pools are comparatively small and clean. Spread a warm, uneven reflection across adjacent wet slabs and maintain warm masonry illumination near the source.
- Shadows have useful structural shape, but the scene needs more soft contact depth around debris, column feet and wall bases without crushing the black wall detail.

### Materials and details

- Outdoor slabs need irregular large proportions, chipped edges, hairline cracks, subtle height variation and less uniform joints. The current repeated rectangular grid is the dominant material mismatch.
- Wetness is not convincingly distributed: a few bright spots do not reproduce the target's many shallow irregular puddles, streaked highlights and surface roughness variation. Add broad coherent puddle regions and use rain rings within wet areas rather than relying on scattered ambient ring marks.
- Walls appear clean/dark and mechanically coursed. Add weathering concentrated at ledges, exposed breaks and bases, and larger legible stone variation.
- Moss should accumulate on upward-facing rubble and broken wall tops, with darker moisture at ground contacts. E02's debris is predominantly uniform gray and lacks the target's green embedded appearance.
- Debris needs a hierarchy from large fallen blocks through medium angular fragments to small chips; do not distribute a uniform narrow strip of similarly sized crumbs.
- The very bright white broken-looking patches on the interior floor near the right wall need material/geometry inspection: they read closer to pasted flat pale patches than grounded broken slabs.
- Rain remains visible, but static stills cannot establish temporal naturalness. Match droplet/ripple scale to slabs, and keep the character silhouette and UI free from excessive weather noise.

## Snow hero342 — detailed findings

Target: `.dream-loop/continuous-world/concept-snow.png`.

- **Composition:** The diagonal packed path, traveler position, framing trees and major rock group placement are close to the original. The image still differs in some tree crown proportions and the path's internal texture, but wholesale layout replacement is unnecessary for this hero.
- **Lighting:** Cold white-blue light and long tree shadows are close. E02's snow highlights are somewhat flatter and brighter, while shadow edges and character shadow look softer/less grounded in places. Preserve cool ambient light and add local contact depth at boots, tree roots and rock bases.
- **Materials:** The target trees have finer needle/branch silhouettes and fine broken snow coverage. E02 trees have broad jagged plates and conspicuous thick white shards. Improve foliage resolution/geometry and snow masks so white accumulates along upper branches without reading as independent chunks.
- Snow-covered boulders retain the correct layered rock forms, but smooth inflated-looking caps need uneven wind-shaped thickness, finer granular normals and softer transitions into exposed stone. Avoid simply increasing global bump strength.
- The packed path in E02 resembles mottled gravel/ice with repeating granular circles. The target is a subtler flattened, wind-softened snow track. Reduce coarse rock-like detail and build low-contrast compaction, shallow surface undulation and gentle edge blending.
- Snow ground needs subtle nonuniform microtexture and low-amplitude broad mounds without destroying the open smooth areas that define the target.
- Fine details still differ: sparse airborne snow sparkle distribution, footprint/compaction response around the traveler, small snow/rock contact holes and tree silhouette tips. These are secondary to foliage and cap material quality.

## Separate qualitative gates

### Overlook — view-375: FAIL, major visual repair needed

- A straight diagonal terrain boundary across the upper third is visible as a large shading/material discontinuity. Remove the seam by aligning/blending terrain geometry, normals and surface response. Verify under the same camera and neighboring viewpoints.
- The lower-right drop reads as a uniform smooth wall with a hard upper edge. Shape a believable ledge and layered exposed cliff, transitioning snow thickness naturally along its lip.
- Large left and right rock masses frame a corridor, but there is little legible distant vista or layered depth to justify an overlook. Clarify near foreground, drop and far landscape with actual depth, atmospheric separation and a clear opening in the framing.
- Some small boulders look awkwardly perched or disconnected from the ground. Resolve base intersections/contact shadows and build snow accumulation around their feet.
- The endpoint/path continuation is not clearly resolved in this still. Verify the traveled approach, end boundary and camera behavior separately; do not infer usability from this capture.

### Rainwood transition — view-120: NEEDS IMPROVEMENT; continuity UNPROVEN

- The broad route is readable, but the frame is a straight road with repeated similar trees and scattered identical low plants. Create grouped canopy masses, varied trunks/crown heights, irregular undergrowth and occasional substantial side landmarks.
- Ground reads as a uniform tiled brown/green mixture. Add damp soil gradients, leaf litter, root contacts and more irregular road edges; use a few larger vegetation masses rather than simply increasing isolated tuft count.
- Use canopy shadows and controlled atmosphere to introduce depth and a visible approach toward the abbey. This single middle-region still does not show either region boundary or prove continuous light/weather changes.

### Frost ascent transition — view-265: NEEDS IMPROVEMENT; continuity UNPROVEN

- The scene is mostly a cold gray corridor with dark green conifers and large bare rocks; it does not show a persuasive gradual snow accumulation progression within this frame.
- Add patchy retained snow in rock crevices and sheltered ground, mixed branch coverage, exposed wet earth and increasing snow coverage along the route. Do not apply equal snow uniformly to every object.
- Reduce repeated large rock/evergreen pairs and give the ascent a varied opening/closing rhythm, with rock scale tied to the route's grade and side slopes.
- Improve contact grounding for raised-looking boulders and conifers. The right rock formation is very dominant and needs to participate in a larger landform rather than read as an isolated stack.
- Rain streaks/rings remain visible in this capture. Inspect whether their transition into snow is intentional and spatially gradual during actual travel. No motion/weather continuity verdict can be made from this still.
