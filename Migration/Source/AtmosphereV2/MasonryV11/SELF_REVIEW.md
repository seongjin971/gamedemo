# Stair V11 source self review

Status: ready for a native integration comparison, not whole-scene visual acceptance.

The direct before/after clay inspection confirms the important structural change: the old flight assembled 90 risers, 90 treads and 98 extra lips with aligned column seams; the new flight contains 90 closed single-piece stones whose vertical seams visibly alternate between rows. There is no separate ridge or second thin strip at each front edge. The original total tread width is read from the bridge, ten nominal level heights are retained, and the fixed support/landing/cheek assemblies are not selected for removal. Stair stone faces use one material slot, so top/front tint separation is no longer baked into the asset assembly.

The first render was rejected because it looked like excessively clean new blocks and its boolean-generated material slots also caused selected blocks to render white. It is preserved in `rejected-clean-pass1`. The final pass clears material slots explicitly, increases thirty broad front-nose losses to 2.6-4.9 cm depth and 2.2-4.5 cm vertical loss, adds fifteen unequal open corner fractures, and introduces restrained shallow walking wear and broad front-face concavity. Hard fracture boundaries remain split; smooth local wear does not introduce a uniformly rounded shoulder. The final neutral preview is materially consistent across the stones and has visible small, uneven front-edge losses.

The final geometry remains deliberately quiet and substantially planar. At the full-flight clay distance, small wear is subtle and these are still cut masonry blocks; this preview does not establish that native photographic material will reach the desired aged-stone reading. The native material must carry believable grain/roughness, and root should explicitly reject or revise the integration if the blocks look freshly poured or lose useful surface damage compared with candidate36. This is a structural replacement candidate rather than a claim that subtle chips alone clear Tier3. The source solves the split assembly and aligned joint causes; native fire, color and material response remain to be checked.

The direct reference inspection also shows that the target stairs retain distinct horizontal levels. The objective is to remove the artificial separate brown strip and repeated projecting lip, not to erase all stair-level shading. No whole-frame color grading was performed here.

Final native export: 63,224 triangles, 52,382 vertices, 90 closed stones. Blender welded-surface checks report zero nonmanifold edges. Independent standard-Python validation reports all 63,224 triangles with strictly positive geometric-cross-dot summed corner normals, zero degenerate triangles, finite arrays and valid indices. Maximum normal length error is 1.28e-7. The original bridge SHA and all 278 removal identities, materials and transform/color tuples match exactly. Unity basis is world-space x/y/z with identity GameObject transform; SHA256 is `5d8b883677e4d23c1eca6f16fb2658f885ab2ee458314ee330d8c109c4580f11`.

Only new MasonryV11 sources and its scratch directory were written. Blender deprecated `Material.use_nodes` warnings are in the scratch log; both final renders and `STAIRS_V11_COMPLETE 90 63224` completed, followed by normal Blender quit. No Unity import, native render, frame-rate measurement, independent scene score or user adoption is claimed.
